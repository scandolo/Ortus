Privacy policy

Last updated 26 November 2026

The short version

  • Ortus has no accounts. It runs on your Mac, and your settings stay there.
  • We never see your messages, the websites you visit, or your AI chats.
  • The browser extension talks only to the Ortus app on your Mac. It sends nothing over the internet.
  • The Mac app sends anonymous usage events, like "focus session started", so we can see what works. It's on if you leave it ticked when you set up Ortus, and you can turn it off anytime in Settings.
  • The homepage and the welcome page count visits with one anonymous cookie, never used for ads. You can switch it off.
  • We don't sell data, show ads, or share data with anyone for marketing.

Who we are

Ortus is made by Federico Scandolara, an independent developer based in Italy, who is responsible for the data described here. Questions or requests: support@ortusmode.com.

The Mac app

Stored only on your Mac

  • Your settings, modes, schedules and the state of your current session, in your macOS preferences.
  • If you connect Slack: your Slack sign-in, in your macOS login keychain. It is not synced to iCloud.
  • The list of websites to block during a session, which the browser extension reads.

To remove all of it, disconnect Slack in Ortus Settings, then delete Ortus, the ~/Library/Application Support/Ortus folder, and ~/Library/Preferences/com.ortusmode.app.plist.

Anonymous usage events

If you leave "Share anonymous usage" ticked when you finish the welcome page, or turn it on in Ortus → Settings, the app sends short events to PostHog, our analytics provider, in its EU cloud. They tell us whether Ortus works and how much it's used: Ortus was running that day; a focus session started or ended (started by hand or by a schedule, which built-in mode, how long it was planned for and how long it lasted, how many apps and websites it blocks, how many times Ortus closed a blocked app, and whether it ended on time, was cancelled in the grace period or used the weekly emergency end); a session was extended or locked early; the browser extension connected in a browser; a mode or schedule was saved or deleted; the Ortus panel was opened; an update was installed; a chat message was sent; Slack was connected.

Each event carries a random ID for your installation (not linked to your name or email), the Ortus and macOS versions, your Mac's model, language and screen size, and a few counts, such as how many days a schedule covers. If you leave usage sharing on when you finish the welcome page, the app uses that browser's website visitor ID (see "This website" below) instead of its own, so we can see how people go from the website to using the app. Like any internet request, an event also reveals your IP address, which PostHog uses to estimate a rough location and then discards.

Events never include message contents, the names of the websites or apps you block or visit, your Slack data, or what you ask in chat.

Nothing is sent until usage sharing is on, and turning it off in Settings stops it straight away. Earlier versions sent fewer events to PostHog in the United States, some of them by default; email us if you'd like those deleted.

Updates

Ortus checks ortusmode.com for new versions, which points it to GitHub to download them. Both receive your IP address as part of those requests: ortusmode.com through Vercel, as described below, and GitHub under its privacy statement.

Slack (optional)

If you connect Slack, Ortus uses your sign-in only to set your Slack status and Do Not Disturb when a session starts and ends. It talks directly to Slack; nothing goes through us.

Chat (optional)

Chat runs your own Claude Code on your Mac. Your questions and the answers travel between Claude Code and Anthropic under your own Claude account and Anthropic's terms, and any connected tools (such as Slack or Gmail) are the ones you set up in Claude. Ortus doesn't send your chats to us and doesn't keep them after you quit. Claude Code may keep its own history on your Mac.

The browser extension

The Ortus extension for Chrome, Arc, Edge and Brave has one job: block the websites you chose in the Ortus app while a focus session is on.

  • It gets your block list from the Ortus app on your Mac, through the browser's native messaging. It makes no internet requests.
  • To block a site, it compares the address of the pages you open with your block list, on your device. It doesn't record, store or send your browsing history.
  • It stores, in your browser only: whether it's connected to Ortus, a random ID that tells your browser profiles apart, and the current session's rules, which are cleared when the browser closes.
  • It has no analytics and no ads.

The use of information received from Chrome APIs adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

This website

ortusmode.com is hosted by Vercel, which processes standard request data, such as IP address and browser type, to deliver and protect the site.

The homepage and the welcome page also send anonymous events to PostHog, in its EU cloud, so we can see whether they work. On the homepage: the page was viewed or left, which sections were reached, how far it was scrolled, and which Download button was clicked. On the welcome page, which Ortus opens once after you install it: that it was opened, which setup steps you saw, whether you clicked to add the browser extension, and whether you turned on usage sharing when you finished. These are the website's events, separate from the app's usage events above, so the welcome page sends them whatever you choose there.

Both pages set one first-party cookie holding a random ID, so a return visit counts as the same visitor instead of a new one. The cookie lasts a year and isn't linked to your name or email. If you leave usage sharing on when you finish the welcome page, the Mac app uses the same ID, as described above. PostHog also keeps a few entries for the open tab, which go away when you close it. Like any request, these events reveal your IP address, which PostHog uses to estimate a rough location and then discards. To stop being counted in a browser, open ortusmode.com/?notrack in it. The site's other pages have no analytics.

Email

Mail to support@ortusmode.com is forwarded by Cloudflare to our inbox. We use it only to reply to you.

Legal basis and how long we keep data

We send anonymous usage events on the basis of our legitimate interest in knowing whether Ortus works and how it's used, and only once usage sharing is on; you can turn it off anytime in Settings. We count visits to the homepage and the welcome page on the basis of our legitimate interest in knowing whether the site works; you can switch it off with the link above or block it with any content blocker. We keep usage events and website events for up to 24 months, and support emails for as long as needed to help you.

Your rights

You can ask to access or delete data about you, or object to its use, by emailing support@ortusmode.com. Because usage events aren't linked to your identity, we may need your installation ID to find them. You can also complain to your data protection authority; in Italy that's the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.

Children

Ortus isn't directed at children under 16.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. For significant changes, we'll also say so in the app.
