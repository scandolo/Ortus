Why we built Ortus

Distraction is the default now. You open Slack, X or email on muscle memory, for one quick check. Blink, and the morning is gone. The one thing that mattered hasn't moved.

Every fix we tried was too soft or too dumb. Do Not Disturb, Focus modes and Slack statuses mute the noise but leave the app one click away, and almost half of all interruptions are ones we start ourselves.1 Hard blockers do hold, but they also lock you out of the message or file you need to get the work done, so they end up switched off.

So we built one that holds, and still lets you work.

1. Gloria Mark, University of California, Irvine: 49% of interruptions are self-interruptions.

For people who make things

There's a war foryour attention.Everyone around youis losing it.

Some of the smartest teams in the world spend their days making feeds, autoplay and red badges harder to put down. And it works. Look around. Everyone is distracted, all day, everywhere.

People like us get our purpose from making things, and very little from consuming them. Losing the long, quiet hours that making needs is an existential threat.

Ortus kills the distractions and gets you what you need from your apps without opening them, until the work is done.

Questions

Before youlock in.

What if something urgent happens?

Almost nothing is so urgent it can't wait two hours. If you need to check, ask your agent. Once a week you also get an emergency end.

I need Slack for the work itself.

Ask Claude Code for the message or the file. It takes seconds, and you never see the rest of Slack.

No end button is too extreme.

That's the point. If you could stop it, you would. You get a few seconds to back out when a session starts.

What about my phone?

Ortus guards your Mac, where the work happens. Put the phone in another room. iPhone is on the roadmap.

I don't use Claude Code.

The blocker works fully without it. Only the chat needs your own Claude Code.

Does Ortus read my messages?

It runs on your Mac with no account and no server of ours. When you ask a question, your own Claude Code does the reading.

Is it really free?

Yes. Ortus is free and open source, and there is no account to create.
